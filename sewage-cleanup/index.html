Few emergencies in a building are as hazardous as sewage backing up into it. Raw sewage contains dangerous bacteria, viruses, parasites and other pathogens, including E. coli, hepatitis, salmonella and giardia, that pose serious health risks to anyone who touches contaminated materials. Lehi Restoration provides emergency sewage cleanup throughout Lehi, responding 24/7 with properly trained technicians, appropriate personal protective equipment and EPA-registered disinfectants to decontaminate your property safely.

The industry classifies sewage as black water, or Category 3, which is the most dangerous and most contaminated type of water damage there is. This classification requires the most stringent cleanup protocols, including full personal protective equipment, containment barriers, removal of all porous materials that contacted sewage and thorough disinfection of every remaining surface. Our technicians follow IICRC S500 standards for Category 3 water damage so your property is restored to safe occupancy standards.

Here is what the work covers: standing sewage is pumped out, contaminated water is extracted from the structure, porous materials the sewage touched (drywall, insulation, carpet and padding) are torn out and disposed of properly, hard surfaces are disinfected thoroughly, antimicrobials are applied, and the structure is dried so mold does not follow. To finish, thermal foggers and hydroxyl generators of industrial grade deodorize the space so the sewage smell does not linger.

Warning Signs for Sewage Cleanup

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Strong sewage or rotten egg odors coming from drains or lower levels
  • Wet spots on floors or walls accompanied by foul odors
  • Carpet or flooring contaminated when a toilet overflowed with solid waste
  • Several fixtures backing up at once instead of only one
  • Running a sink or washer and hearing a toilet or nearby drain gurgle
  • Sewage or dark water backing up through floor drains, toilets or tubs
  • Every sink, tub and shower draining slowly even after you plunge them

Why Professional Sewage Cleanup Matters

No homeowner should try to clean up sewage personally. The biological hazards in raw sewage require specialized training, proper personal protective equipment (Tyvek suits, respirators, protective gloves and boots) and EPA-registered disinfection products that are not available to consumers. People exposed to the pathogens in sewage can become seriously ill with skin and respiratory infections, hepatitis or gastrointestinal disease.

Contaminated materials count as biohazard waste, and disposing of them properly is part of a professional cleanup. Putting carpet, insulation or drywall soaked with sewage out with household trash breaks the law and endangers sanitation workers and your community. Licensed biohazard disposal facilities take this waste from certified restoration firms, which keep the required protocols in place.

Sewage Cleanup Prevention Tips

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Find your sewer cleanout now and check its condition, so it can be reached in an emergency
  • Fit a strainer over each sink and shower drain so hair and debris stay out of the pipes
  • Food waste, oils and grease block pipes, so put them in the trash and not the sink
  • Have roots removed where trees grow by the sewer, before they get inside the pipe and plug it
  • Toilet paper is the only thing to flush, and that rules out wipes sold as flushable, which still clog
  • Have your sewer line inspected with a camera every 2 to 3 years to catch potential problems
