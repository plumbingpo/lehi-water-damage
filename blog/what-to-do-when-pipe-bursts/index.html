What to Do When a Pipe Bursts in Lehi, UT

Home / Blog / What to Do When a Pipe Bursts in Lehi, UT

A burst pipe is one of the most stressful plumbing emergencies a homeowner can experience. Water pours out of a burst line at a rate of 4 to 8 gallons a minute, so within hours rooms may be flooded, possessions damaged, and building materials weakened. What you do in the first few minutes after finding the break often decides whether you face a simple repair or a full restoration, and it can keep thousands of dollars of damage off the bill. This guide from Lehi Restoration gives Lehi, UT homeowners a clear action plan for this common emergency.

Step 1: Shut Off the Water Supply Immediately

Your first job after a pipe breaks is simple: get the water shut off as soon as you possibly can. Damage and repair bills grow with each minute the leak runs. Here's what to do: locate your main water shutoff valve. Look by the water meter first, since that is where Lehi houses tend to have it, often in a utility room, garage, or basement, or on a street-side exterior wall. Rotating it clockwise, the old righty-tighty rule, cuts water to the whole home. If you can identify the specific pipe that burst and it has its own shutoff valve nearby, close that valve as well.

Can't find the valve, or can't get it to turn? Phone the water utility right away and ask them to stop service at the meter. It is also why every homeowner should learn where the shutoff is long before anything goes wrong. Find yours today and give it a test turn to confirm it moves freely. A valve that will not move should be repaired by a plumber now, not during a flood.

Step 3: Protect Your Property and Belongings

Before the crew arrives, you can cut your losses, starting with getting documents, electronics, and valuables out of the wet zone. Slip plastic blocks or aluminum foil beneath furniture legs so nothing sits directly on soaked carpet. Pick up any area rugs lying on wet flooring. Where puddles are easy to get to, blot them up with towels. Air needs to circulate, so leave cabinets and closets open. If you can reach the breaker panel safely and stay out of the water, switch off power to the affected rooms.

Never walk through standing water near electrical outlets, appliances, or panels. When flooding is heavy and there is no safe route to the panel, do not try to shut the power off; let professionals evaluate it first. Your safety matters more than the house.

Step 4: Record the Damage for Your Claim

Hold off on cleanup until you have a thorough record of the damage to give your insurer. Shoot video and photos showing the standing water, every damaged material, and any belongings that were hit. If the spot where the pipe failed is visible, record it, along with the day and approximate hour you first noticed the problem. This documentation, combined with the detailed reports Lehi Restoration provides, will support your insurance claim and help you seek fair compensation for the damage.

How Lehi Homeowners Can Avoid Burst Pipes

While not all pipe bursts can be prevented, Lehi homeowners can significantly reduce their risk by insulating exposed pipes in unheated areas like garages, attics, and crawl spaces. When temperatures drop, let faucets drip a little so water keeps moving in the lines. Open cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls to allow warm air to reach pipes. Pressure over 80 PSI calls for a pressure reducing valve, so check your reading from time to time. Replace aging supply lines, especially rubber washing machine hoses, with braided stainless steel. Above all, make sure you can find your main shutoff long before an emergency.
