Of all the stages in restoring water damage, drying the structure counts most, and it is the stage most often botched by people without training. Extraction takes care of standing water, but drywall, framing, subflooring, insulation, concrete and other building materials stay loaded with trapped moisture. A structure left wet ends up with odors, wood rot, mold and failing materials, so the moisture has to be removed in a scientific way using dehumidification and controlled evaporation.

The structural drying technicians at Lehi Restoration are IICRC-certified and trained in psychrometry, the science of how temperature, humidity and moisture in air relate. For each job they apply it to calculate airflow patterns, temperature settings and the placement of equipment that will give the best conditions for evaporation. Plaster, concrete, hardwood and drywall each dry at their own rate, so each needs its own approach to get down to an acceptable moisture level with no added damage.

Every day of the drying process, we check moisture using thermal imaging cameras, thermo-hygrometers and calibrated moisture meters. Readings are recorded from start to finish in a detailed drying log, which shows moisture dropping step by step in every affected material. Each day we use those numbers to reposition or retune the equipment so drying stays efficient. A surface that feels dry to your hand proves nothing, so the job is finished only once all affected materials test within the acceptable standards set by IICRC S500.

What to Expect From Our Structural Drying

Our systematic approach delivers quality results on every project. Here's how we handle structural drying from start to finish:

1

Moisture Assessment

We build a complete moisture map of every affected area with hygrometers, thermal imaging and moisture meters. Baseline readings come from the map, and so does the plan for placing equipment.

2

Setting the Equipment

Guided by the moisture map, we place high-velocity air movers and commercial LGR dehumidifiers where they will do the most good. We tune airflow and temperature to suit the materials that need drying.

3

Daily Checks and Adjustments

Our technician returns daily to take moisture readings at all monitoring points, adjust equipment placement and document drying progress. You receive updates on the drying timeline.

4

Verifying the Dry Standard

Once every affected material reads at an acceptable moisture level, we record the final numbers, pull the equipment and certify the property as meeting dry standard.

What a Professional Brings to Structural Drying

Structural drying is a science, not a guessing game. Simply placing fans and hoping for the best is the most common cause of secondary mold damage after water events. To do it professionally, you have to know how each material takes on and gives up moisture, work out the grain depression, meaning the moisture removal capacity, for the job, and check progress every day. Get it wrong in one direction and mold feeds on leftover moisture, get it wrong in the other and wood or similar materials are damaged by over-drying.

Consumer products and the equipment professionals dry structures with have very little in common. High-velocity air movers produce precise airflow patterns that speed up evaporation from the structure, and low-grain refrigerant (LGR) commercial dehumidifiers capture 10 to 20 times the moisture a home unit can. Between the investment in equipment and the training needed to run it, it is no surprise that DIY drying falls well short of a professional job.

Maintenance Tips for Structural Drying

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Deal with water intrusion right away, since an early start on drying gives a better result
  • Do not place personal items on top of drying equipment or block the airflow from air movers
  • Tell your restoration technician immediately if you notice new wet areas or odors during drying
  • Plan for daily access so readings can be taken and equipment adjusted
  • Every hour of run time matters, so resist switching the machines off for some quiet
  • Let air movers and dehumidifiers run continuously, 24/7, until the technician removes them

When to Call for Structural Drying in Lehi

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Wall finishes that start to peel or bubble in the days after water damage
  • Earlier water damage that was never professionally dried and now shows deterioration
  • Materials that feel damp or cool to the touch even after visible water is gone
  • Drywall that is soft, swollen or shows moisture staining
  • Wood flooring with boards that cup, crown or pull apart
  • Condensation forming on windows in the area that was wet
  • A stale, musty smell that lingers even though the water was extracted
