Dealing With Leaking Pipe Water Damage in Lehi

A leaking pipe rarely announces itself. The drip is hidden in a wall cavity, a sink cabinet or beneath the slab, so the first clue tends to be a stain, a spongy patch of floor or an oddly high water bill. By then the materials around the pipe have often been wet for weeks. Our team in Lehi finds how far the moisture has spread and dries all of it, not just the part you can see.

The plumbing repair stops the water, but it does not remove what has already soaked in. Getting it out is the work we do. With meters and a thermal camera we locate every wet material, make only the openings required, then keep drying equipment running until moisture levels read normal again. Because slow leaks give mold time to start, we also check for growth and treat affected surfaces.

Do This First

Before We Arrive

  1. Shut Off the WaterFor a leak at a toilet or sink, turn off the small supply valve feeding that fixture. When the source is unclear, the house main is the valve to turn off.
  2. Stay Clear of ElectricityIf water is near outlets, switches or a light fixture, switch off the breaker for that area. Keep your hands off cords and devices that have gotten wet.
  3. Read the MeterWith every tap off, look at the meter. Movement on the dial means water is escaping out of sight, perhaps beneath the slab.
  4. Clear Out and Take PhotosEmpty the closet or cabinet by the leak, then photograph the pipe, any stains and any warped material before repairs start or items are discarded.
  5. Have the Area MeteredHave the area measured with meters before it is closed up. Inside the cavity, moisture can remain even when the wall surface feels dry.
The Usual Culprits

How It Usually Starts

  • Soil movement and rubbing that wear a hole in a line buried below the slab.
  • A nail or screw driven into a pipe during a remodel or while hanging something on the wall.
  • Cold snaps that freeze piping in unheated spaces, leaving small cracks that leak after the thaw.
  • Drain fittings and P-traps below sinks that work loose and leak whenever water goes down.
  • Supply connections and compression fittings behind toilets or under sinks that have worn out or worked loose.
Why Speed Matters

Where the Damage Spreads

Damaged Flooring

Water from a slab or wall leak travels under flooring. Hardwood cups, laminate swells at the seams, and adhesive under vinyl and tile lets go.

Decayed Wall Framing

After weeks of dampness, bottom plates and wall studs start to go soft and rot. Any piece too weak to carry load gets cut out and swapped for new lumber.

Hidden Mold Growth

Nothing suits mold better than the dark, moist space inside a wall. With a slow leak it often covers the back of the drywall before a single spot shows on the painted side.

Soaked Wall Insulation

Like a sponge, wall insulation takes in water from the pipe and keeps the framing next to it damp. It dries very slowly in a closed cavity, so saturated insulation is usually pulled out.

Coverage

What About Insurance?

A slow leak and a sudden one are not handled the same way by insurance. Policies often cover damage that is sudden and accidental. A leak lasting weeks or months usually falls outside that, and certain policies name it as an exclusion. A hidden leak you had no way of knowing about may be handled more favorably than one that was visible and ignored. Check your policy and ask your agent. So the adjuster can work from solid facts, we provide photos, moisture readings and notes on the leak source.

Pricing

What Affects the Cost

  • The number of rooms and floors affected, such as a ceiling underneath the leaking line.
  • Which finishes and flooring got wet, as some can be saved by drying and some cannot.
  • Where the leak is, since an open cabinet costs less than a wall or slab.
  • What has to be torn out and put back, from drywall and insulation to cabinets and flooring.
