How Logan Homeowners Can Avoid Water Damage

Most of the water damage we see in Logan homes started with something small that gave warning first. A few minutes of checking a couple of times a year catches most of it.

Begin at the hoses. The rubber hoses on washing machines, dishwashers, toilets and ice makers wear out, and braided stainless replacements are inexpensive. Look under every sink for stains, swollen cabinet floors or a musty smell. Check the water heater for rust at the base and drain a little from the tank once a year to clear sediment. Clean gutters and downspouts that discharge well away from the house keep rain out of the foundation.

Find the main water shutoff now, and show every adult in the home how to close it. Small battery leak alarms near the washing machine, water heater and sinks give you an early warning for very little money. A stain that spreads or a floor that feels soft is worth a call, and Logan homeowners are welcome to ask us about it.

Restoration Challenges Logan Homeowners Face

Ask anyone on our crew what fills the schedule in Logan and you will hear the same six answers. They are all common, and with every one of them waiting adds to the repair.

Mold: Most mold growth is the follow-up to an earlier leak that seemed to be taken care of. Surfaces dry with towels and a box fan, yet the pad, subfloor and inside of the wall hold moisture. Growth can begin within 24 to 48 hours, then spread quietly until you smell it or see spotting.

Hoses: The braided or rubber lines behind toilets, sinks and washing machines are under pressure all day. A burst connector keeps spraying until somebody closes a valve, and that may take a long while in an empty house. Replacing old connectors and knowing where your shutoff sits are the cheapest protection available.

Pumps: Sump pumps, where a home has one, tend to fail at the worst moment after years of running without attention. Stuck float switches, tripped breakers and burned-out motors are what we find most. The result is several inches of water over the lowest floor, soaking stored boxes, flooring and the bottom of the walls. A battery backup and a quick test before the rainy part of the year go a long way.

Toilets: Among bathroom emergencies in Logan, the toilet that will not stop rising tops the list. It matters where the water came from, because tank or supply water is clean and used bowl water is not. Either way it moves under tile edges and vanity cabinets, so the floor needs to be checked with a meter.

Cooling: Your air conditioner drains condensation through a line that most owners never see. If the line plugs or the pan rusts through, water drips into the ceiling or the floor around the unit. Because it happens slowly, the damage in many Logan homes is well along before a stain appears. Having the line flushed each year, before the cooling season, prevents most of these.

Flooding: When storm drains cannot keep up with a downpour, surface water heads for the lowest doorway it can find. Garages, walkout basements and ground-floor rooms at the low side of the lot flood first. Our Lehi Restoration crews extract it, remove what cannot be cleaned and dry the structure to verified readings.

The First Hour After Water Damage in Logan

What you should do before we arrive depends on what kind of water you are looking at. Clean water from a supply pipe or a tap left running is the least risky. You can walk in it with shoes on, soak it up, and move belongings out of it. Water from a washing machine, dishwasher or aquarium is dirtier, so wear gloves and wash your hands afterward. Sewage and floodwater from outdoors are a health hazard, and the only job for you is to close the door and keep everyone out.

Whatever the type, a few things help our Logan crew start faster. Clear a path from the door to the wet area and move cars so the truck can park close. Write down when you found the water and what you think caused it. Find your insurance policy number if it is easy to get to.

Time matters because clean water does not stay clean. After it has sat in building materials it degrades, and mold can take hold in 24 to 48 hours. That is why the opening hour counts, even when a Logan leak seems small.
